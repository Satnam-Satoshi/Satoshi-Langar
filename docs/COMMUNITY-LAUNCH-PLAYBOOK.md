# Community launch playbook

Prepared September 30, 2026. This pack makes the next decisions concrete. It does not claim that social accounts, partnerships, events, payment automation or a daily publishing operation already exist. Read the [ecosystem plan](ECOSYSTEM-PLAN.md) and [roadmap](../ROADMAP.md) first.

## The founder's steps, in order

1. **Review the website preview and expanded plan.** Confirm the story and navigation before the next public release. satnam.x stays deferred until you explicitly resume it.
2. **Choose the account owner and recovery route.** Provide a dedicated project email address, then sign in to the selected platforms yourself when needed. A backup human should be able to recover community accounts. Never send passwords, recovery phrases or private keys through a public issue or chat.
3. **Register and verify the first channels.** Use the proposed account pack below, subject to name availability. Keep ownership with the project; record public URLs only after successful registration. Approve the biography and first welcome post before it is sent.
4. **Name three initial human stewards.** We need community/moderation, editorial, and a local Langar organizer. One person may cover more than one role initially, with a backup and capacity limits recorded. All roles remain unfilled until accepted.
5. **Choose a local pilot context.** Give the organizer a city/region and the real kitchen or venue relationship to explore. Keep private contacts out of GitHub. An introduction is not an agreement; the organizer must confirm local responsibilities before invitations.
6. **Review donation activation separately.** Confirm the intended recipient and purpose, native BTC and LTC setup, human custodian, accounting reviewer and recovery. The founder reaffirmed the BTC/LTC addresses from the prior LTC draft for this website revision; static donation requests are published after address-format checks. No test transfer or cryptographic control proof was performed. Complete backup custody, receipt reconciliation and reporting before describing a managed donation operation.
7. **Accept one first publication and one outreach batch.** The editor presents an original LTC edition with sources. The communications steward presents named recipients and exact draft messages for permission to send. Approve only the concrete items you want published.
8. **Review weekly evidence.** Look at completed contributions, sources/corrections, costs if any, operational blockers and next decisions. Continue, narrow or stop each pilot according to evidence.

Steps 2, 4 and 5 unlock operations; they need people and account access, not more page design. No paid upgrade is assumed. The [agent team](AGENT-TEAM.md) can prepare drafts and checks while these decisions are pending.

## Moderation and account operations

Publish a short welcome/rules message: dignity across backgrounds; no harassment, scams, impersonation, unsolicited financial promotions or requests for keys; respect consent and creator rights. Moderators should remove harmful material using a recorded process and offer a way to appeal. The private reporting route must be tested before opening a large public room; do not route sensitive incidents into GitHub issues.

Human administrators hold account recovery and signing capability. Agents draft content and summarize permitted public feedback. Give the smallest role needed, separate content drafting from publishing, review inactive access and retain only necessary moderation records. Enable available strong authentication and store recovery material under human control. Screenshots and public documentation must not expose backup codes or credentials.

Before adding a channel link, check: correct owner, display name, biography, official repository/site backlink, working invite, visible rules, reporting route and backup administrator. Record the reviewer and date in the release evidence. A profile's existence alone is not a functioning community.

## Bitcoin meetups: first session pack

**Proposed session:** Learn, create, serve — a Bitcoin beginner circle. No event date or location is announced by this template.

- 10 minutes: welcome, consent and why no wallet is needed.
- 20 minutes: Sikh Bitcoin introduction and a no-money verification exercise.
- 15 minutes: one artist or open-source contributor explains a useful piece of work.
- 15 minutes: a human organizer describes a possible local service need.
- 10 minutes: choose one voluntary next step and offer feedback.

The host supplies venue permission, capacity, accessibility, local safety review, code of conduct, contact, cancellation plan and any necessary age/safeguarding arrangements. Photography is separately opt-in. Do not publish attendance numbers or participant stories without evidence and consent. Gather only necessary registration information and state retention. A future kitchen visit requires its own local approval.

Start with people who have a relevant reason to care: Bitcoin educators, maintainers of tools we propose to use, local meetup organizers, artists, gurdwara/community-kitchen stewards and AI projects interested in bounded public work. Record why each proposed introduction is useful. Never scrape member lists, buy contact lists or automate bulk direct messages.

## What the first public review should show

A newcomer should find a lesson, understand the program map, see how a human or agent can contribute, know which channels are verified, and distinguish original published journalism from drafts. An artist should understand the proposed payment path. A supporter should see either a verified approved checkout or an honest explanation that financial intake is not yet available.

Release evidence includes the exact commit and preview URL, mobile/keyboard checks, link checks, build result, source notes, open limitations and rollback instructions. Public claims follow verified behavior. Preserve the earlier documentation and [decision history](DECISIONS.md); new design work must not erase what we already published.
