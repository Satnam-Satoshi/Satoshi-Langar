## Problem

Lunch Time Conversations was represented as a grid of future formats. It described a publication but did not yet behave like one, and the “daily newspaper” label risked implying an operating cadence before the editorial process was ready.

## Options considered

1. Retain the format-card directory and add articles later.
2. Present simulated headlines to make the publication appear active.
3. Build the permanent editorial frame now, clearly mark its pre-publication state, and let verified editions populate it after human review.

## Decision

Adopt a publication-first model with a restrained editorial masthead, one lead story, four permanent desks, four article formats, and a visible human–AI newsroom workflow. The founding edition remains explicitly in editorial review. No date, frequency, article, or source is represented as published before approval.

## Reasoning

A trusted newspaper is defined by judgment, evidence, and correction behavior—not by the number of cards on a page. Establishing the editorial contract before the first edition makes the product useful to readers and operationally clear to future contributors without fabricating activity.

## Benefits

- Creates a recognizable, premium publication identity.
- Establishes clear editorial coverage without claiming exhaustive expertise.
- Makes human accountability for AI-assisted research visible.
- Preserves an honest pre-publication state.
- Provides a durable structure for future briefings, essays, conversations, and source files.

## Tradeoffs

- Version 1 shows one edition in review rather than a populated archive.
- The publication will not promise a daily cadence until the newsroom can sustain it.
- Editorial serif typography adds a distinct product voice that must remain limited to publication surfaces.

## Future review date

Review after the first three Founder-approved editions or before establishing a formal publishing cadence, whichever occurs first.
