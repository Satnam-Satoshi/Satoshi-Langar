## 1. Constitutional authority

The Satnam Satoshi Community Operating System (SSCOS) is the permanent institutional system of record for the Satnam Satoshi mission. The SSCOS Blueprint v0.1 is the foundational constitutional draft. Version 0.2 carries that authority forward by establishing controlled evolution, document governance, decision records, subsystem charters, and agent charters.

This baseline does not replace or redesign the founding architecture. It consolidates the known constitutional requirements into a controlled, reviewable form. No repository document, software component, operational procedure, agent instruction, or external platform may create a conflicting constitution or parallel governance system.

If a subordinate artifact conflicts with this baseline or an approved successor, the approved constitutional text governs. Until this baseline is approved, SSCOS Blueprint v0.1 remains the foundational authority and this document remains a proposed clarification.

## 2. Mission

SSCOS exists to help people coordinate knowledge, service, sovereignty, technology, and shared resources through transparent institutions and accountable tools. Its mission is to create durable public-interest infrastructure grounded in truth, seva, human dignity, community stewardship, responsible innovation, and verifiable rules.

SSCOS shall enable human communities and carefully bounded artificial-intelligence agents to collaborate without surrendering human authority, privacy, custody, conscience, or the right to meaningful review.

## 3. Foundational doctrine

### 3.1 Truth

Material claims shall be traceable to sources, dates, methods, and confidence. Estimates and interpretations shall be distinguished from confirmed facts. Corrections shall be visible and auditable.

### 3.2 Seva

Institutional capability shall serve people and community purpose. Commercial activity may sustain the mission but shall not silently displace it.

### 3.3 Sovereignty

Humans retain custody, consent, final institutional authority, and emergency control. SSCOS shall not require disclosure of private keys, seed phrases, or similarly sensitive credentials.

### 3.4 Open and durable institutions

Rules, interfaces, decisions, and accountability shall be documented with the rigor expected of enduring technical and public-interest institutions. Designs should favor interoperability, reviewability, portability, and clear succession.

### 3.5 Separation of duties

No person or agent should unilaterally collect evidence, approve conclusions, publish material claims, and control funds where independent checks are reasonably available. Treasury, publishing, security, governance, and operational permissions shall remain appropriately separated.

### 3.6 Privacy and minimum necessary access

SSCOS shall collect and retain only what is necessary for an authorized mission. Access shall be purpose-bound, least-privileged, reviewable, and revocable.

### 3.7 Safety before scale

Architecture, security, privacy, legal, and governance controls precede production implementation. High-impact capabilities require staged testing, explicit limits, monitoring, and human authorization.

### 3.8 Constitutional continuity

SSCOS evolves through versioned amendments and recorded decisions. It shall not be redesigned from scratch when controlled improvement can preserve institutional continuity.

## 4. Institutional roles and authority

### 4.1 Human Founder

Amardeep Baba G Malhotra is the Human Founder and final approver during the founding phase. The Human Founder approves the Constitution, material architectural decisions, implementation authorization, treasury authority, production release, and amendments unless an approved future succession instrument delegates those powers.

### 4.2 Human owners

Every subsystem and agent shall have a named human owner accountable for mission alignment, access review, escalation, incident response, and retirement. Delegation does not remove human accountability.

### 4.3 AI stewards and agents

AI systems are bounded institutional instruments. They possess only explicitly granted permissions, may not expand their own authority, and shall escalate when evidence, policy, safety, or authorization is insufficient. No AI approval substitutes for approval reserved to the Human Founder or another authorized human body.

## 5. System of record and document hierarchy

The authoritative hierarchy is:

1. approved SSCOS Constitution and amendments;
2. approved governance standards and architecture decisions;
3. approved volume and chapter specifications;
4. approved subsystem and agent charters;
5. controlled procedures, registers, and technical documentation;
6. implementation artifacts and operational records.

Google Drive is the controlled institutional document library. `Satnam-Satoshi/Satoshi-Langar` is the authorized GitHub repository for the documentation baseline created under this revision. The repository register records scope and authority. A copy in either system does not become independently authoritative merely by existing; status and approval metadata determine authority.

## 6. Mandatory governance controls

Every controlled document shall include the metadata required by the SSCOS Governance Metadata Standard. Every material architectural decision shall use an Architecture Decision Record containing the problem, options considered, decision, reasoning, benefits, tradeoffs, and future review date.

Every subsystem shall have a charter specifying mission, scope, responsibilities, inputs, outputs, interfaces, dependencies, security, privacy, governance, and success metrics. Every AI agent shall have a charter specifying mission, responsibilities, knowledge sources, permissions, escalation rules, memory, audit trail, human owner, and emergency stop.

## 7. Architecture and implementation gate

Architecture must be documented, reviewed, and approved before implementation begins. A working draft, prototype description, roadmap, or repository presence does not constitute approval. Production code, deployment, custody, trading, payment, treasury, or autonomous transaction authority requires a separate written approval by the Human Founder and any required legal, security, or governance reviewers.

Existing product and agent documents in the repository are subordinate reference material. They may inform future charters and ADRs but do not, by themselves, authorize implementation.

## 8. Amendment, approval, and review

Proposed amendments shall identify the affected version, rationale, dependencies, compatibility impact, tradeoffs, and review date. Amendments remain drafts until approved by the Human Founder during the founding phase. Approved versions shall be immutable in substance; later changes shall create a new version and preserve revision history.

This baseline shall receive its first formal review no later than 2026-09-05, or earlier at the Human Founder's direction. Approval shall be recorded by date, approver, decision, and any conditions.
