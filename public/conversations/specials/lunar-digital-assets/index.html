How Roc Zacharias and Lunar Digital Assets brought a Polygon-era playbook to LitVM, and why Luxxfolio put money behind it.

The clearest link between Lunar and Luxxfolio runs through LitVM. Lunar helped bring the project into the open and is helping recruit builders. Luxxfolio disclosed an investment in it. The public record still leaves two questions unanswered: what Luxxfolio bought, and what LitVM can safely deliver on mainnet.

01 / People

Roc is the through line. The team is the story.

Roc Zacharias’s trajectory is unusually useful for understanding the strategy. A 2024 Polygon governance biography dates his co-founding of Lunar Digital Assets to 2017 and his concentrated support for Matic to a 2019 hackathon connection with Sandeep Nailwal. QuickSwap’s own documentation names Zacharias as a co-founder and LDA CEO alongside a founding team with distinct technical talent, including Nick Mudge and Sameep Singhania. An early QuickSwap AMA records Roc describing Lunar’s role as marketing and community-building advice. Roc later identified himself as a serving Polygon Community Treasury Board member in an Aave governance proposal. These connections show ecosystem work, not that he founded Polygon.

Co-founder / CEO

Roc Zacharias

Co-founded Lunar, QuickSwap and LitVM. LitVM also lists him as a Tesseract adviser. Public X profile.

Strategy / Co-founder

Aztec Amaya

Identified as a Lunar co-founder and strategist by Arizona Tech Week’s speaker profile, and LitVM co-founder in LitVM’s own governance write-up.

There is an overlooked chapter before QuickSwap. In June 2018, trading-software partner HaasOnline named Han Yoon as Lunar CEO and described trading tooling and a planned index fund. The material shows how the company pitched itself at the time, without establishing that the fund ever launched or when the CEO role changed. Amaya’s Arizona Tech Week profile describes Lunar as a full-stack blockchain venture studio. Its practical role is easier to observe than its ownership: the studio has helped projects reach developers and users, and Tesseract makes that work visible today.

03 / Capital

Two public backers. Two different disclosures.

Studio

Lunar Digital Assets

Public unveiling, overlapping operators and a builder accelerator. No verified cap table or ownership relationship with Luxxfolio.

Canadian issuer

Luxxfolio · CSE: LUXX

The issuer says it invested in LitVM. The amount, instrument, recipient legal entity, ownership percentage and rights were not disclosed in the announcement.

US issuer

Lite Strategy · Nasdaq: LITS

Separately disclosed US$1 million initial investment in ZK Innovations, the BVI company identified as LitVM’s developer.

The October announcement says Luxxfolio had made the investment. It does not say it acquired a minority equity stake, obtained a board seat or will receive tokens or fees. An investment announcement does not, by itself, establish equity ownership or operating revenue.

Luxxfolio already mines Litecoin and operates LuxxPool. In its November 12 update, it reported 338 Scrypt miners and estimated 26,838.23 LTC in its treasury as of November 9. The company describes LuxxWallet’s LitVM and MWEB features as in development. For a miner and treasury holder, a programmable ecosystem offers an obvious strategic reason to participate. Whether it produces identifiable revenue for Luxxfolio remains unanswered. Its Greywick/Canton announcement concerns a separate, non-binding letter of intent, not an extension of the LitVM deal.

Lite Strategy’s 10-K records the initial US$1 million SAFE, a right tied to future equity financing, and a separate token warrant. Its November 5 10-Q reports a combined US$1.044 million estimated fair value at September 30 and describes a warrant covering 2% of network tokens if developed. The filed investor deck describes a US$50 million post-money SAFE cap, not a current market valuation. These disclosures belong to Lite Strategy. They tell us nothing about Luxxfolio’s purchase terms or whether a public token exists.

04 / Technology

Litecoin assets, Ethereum settlement

Architecture descriptions can change over time. The current V2 architecture documentation describes Arbitrum Orbit/Nitro execution, Ethereum settlement and a Charms-based route for Litecoin assets. The change matters: Litecoin’s proof of work secures native LTC on Litecoin. It does not, by itself, settle LitVM applications.

  2. Charms / zkLTC
  3. Ethereum
  4. Arbitrum infrastructure

LiteForge is publicly documented as a testnet. LitVM says it migrated from Caldera to Zeeve, and the current configuration lists chain ID 4441. Testnet zkLTC is for testing. It does not demonstrate that a real LTC deposit can be issued as zkLTC or redeemed through the production V2 route. LitVM’s status page leaves the full route’s availability, issuance, redemption, mainnet and integration-specific security information to release documentation.

Read the counter correctly. LitVM’s October 27 post reported 4–5 million testnet transactions on peak days. That is a project-reported peak, not an independent count of current activity. It does not measure unique users, real funds or production revenue.

Charms has public upstream code. That is useful for technical review, but it does not audit the complete LitVM integration or establish how a user would exit if the bridge or network operator stopped functioning. The project describes separate roles for proving and sequencing; it does not provide enough current network-specific evidence to assume those integrations are fully active.

05 / The operating model

Can Lunar repeat its builder playbook?

Tesseract is where Lunar’s role becomes most concrete. LitVM lists the venture studio as a collaborator and describes four kinds of work for selected teams: advisory, marketing, business development and community. The page names project lead Louie Henry H., Lunar chief of staff and LitVM partnerships lead Richard Stangland, Litecoin Foundation APAC lead Robbie Coleman, and advisers Roc Zacharias, Aztec Amaya and Jack Hartley. It says five projects will enter the first track in December; it does not establish five shipped products. That is a program announcement, not proof that those projects have shipped.

At Arizona Tech Week, Amaya described applying what his team learned on Polygon to LitVM. QuickSwap gives this experiment a useful precedent: an exchange needed more than smart contracts; it also needed listings, liquidity, education and a community participants wanted to return to. LitVM is trying to take that operating model into a Litecoin-focused ecosystem. The important test is whether users keep coming back when the initial campaigns and testnet incentives are gone. A small number of working products with repeat users would be stronger evidence than another impressive transaction headline.

06 / The next proof points

What we want to see on the record

  1. Capital: Luxxfolio’s exact instrument, consideration, recipient entity, rights and any separately disclosed direct services relationship with Lunar.
  2. Control: Who can upgrade the bridge and chain, pause or censor transactions, and act if an operator disappears.
  3. Redemption: Released deposit and withdrawal documentation, named deployments, independent audit scope and a demonstrated real-LTC round trip.
  4. Demand: Tesseract projects that ship, unique active users over time, fees actually paid, non-incentivized retention and what those applications solve.

Method: public project documentation, dated first-party announcements, Canadian exchange filings and US SEC filings reviewed through November 19, 2026. Project claims and forward-looking plans are attributed. This is independent editorial research, not investment advice or a live usage audit.
